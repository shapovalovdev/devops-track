Gates

Five points tied to the calendar, not to readiness. The whole point is that they arrive even when it feels too early.

Pack 04: 15–28 Sep
Mock interview #1. The first cold application goes out

Not because you are ready — because a cold application costs nothing. It is calibration: you find out what they actually ask, four months before the answer matters.

Pack 07: 27 Oct–9 Nov
Three decisions you cannot postpone

English — moving or not, and what to do about it. The agent artifact — into the portfolio or not. The harness — you step up a level or you stay. Three months in is the point where you can still change course without losing anything.

Pack 08: 10–23 Nov
The referral channel opens

Not before, and that is deliberate. A referral is spent once: someone you know walks your CV in, and there is no second run at it. You practise where it costs nothing and convert where it counts.

Pack 12: 5–18 Jan
Mock interview #2

Same format as in September. The difference between the two recordings is the measurable result of six months.

Every month
The closed-laptop talk

You explain what you did over the month, with no screen and no notes, and I ask questions. One procedure closes three things at once: interview rehearsal, a check that you understood what you built, and clearance for the next block.

Pack by pack

The order is not arbitrary: every pack stands on what started working in the one before. Jumping ahead is pointless — there would be nothing to stand on.

014–17 Aug
Networking for a specific breakage. First pipelinecompleted

You found the gap yourself. We fix it on a real GitLab and runner failure, not a textbook example — and the first green run shows up along the way.

0218–31 Aug
Pipeline end to end: build → registry → deployin progress

Close the delivery loop. From this point you do not have a lab, you have a small real system — and you can talk about it in a different tone.

031–14 Sep
Prometheus from scratch. Metrics for your own services

You have Loki — that is logs. There are no metrics. Without them there are no meaningful alerts, no SLOs, and no conversation about reliability in an interview.

0415–28 Sep
Alerts in Telegram. First SLO. Traffic generatorgate

On-call has to be felt. A dashboard you have to remember to open is not on-call. This is also when the lab first starts living without you.

0529 Sep–12 Oct
The lab starts breaking on its own. First two postmortems

Here you stop building the system and start operating it. Those are different professions, and hiring is for the second one. For now it only breaks on your days off — the genuinely inconvenient part comes later.

0613–26 Oct
Terraform. First module — by hand

Infrastructure as code is the second most common interview question after CI/CD. You write the first module yourself, without the agent: what you wrote by hand once, you can explain.

0727 Oct–9 Nov
Terraform: modules, remote state, plan/apply in CIgate

The difference between “I have touched Terraform” and “I can use it” is exactly those three things. The three decisions of month three land here too.

0810–23 Nov
Someone else's legacy: you take what you did not build. Ansible lands here toogate

At work nobody hands you a blank page. They hand you someone else's undocumented system and ask you not to break it. That skill does not develop on your own projects — so we will arrange it on purpose.

0924 Nov–7 Dec
Kubernetes: fundamentals, kind/k3s locally. Manifests by hand

K8s is in almost every job ad, but it comes second to last on purpose: without networking, CI/CD and monitoring it turns into a set of incantations you have no way to check.

108–21 Dec
K8s: Helm, ingress, probes. You migrate one service

One real service moved across in full is worth ten tutorials. At the same time the lab moves into phase two — it can now break at night as well.

1122 Dec–4 Jan
Planned unload. Portfolio and README in English

Not “I will rest if it works out”. The unload is written in ahead of time — that is how you get a person back after the holidays instead of losing them entirely. Light work: tidying up what already exists.

125–18 Jan
The lab breaks whenever it likes. Error budgetgate

Now an incident can land mid-shift and you physically cannot respond. That is the lesson: what a system needs so that a three-hour delay is not a catastrophe. This is where the lab starts teaching design rather than debugging.

1319 Jan–1 Feb
Portfolio final: recordings, diagrams, assembling the story

Everything assembles around one claim: “a support engineer who already thinks like an SRE”. It is true, it is hard to fake, and there are not many of them.

What does not change in any pack

Speaking out loud — about an hour

In Russian first, then every second time in English. The cheapest and highest-return task in the whole plan: you have already lost an interview for reasons other than knowledge.

A “why” on every task

If the “why” block did not convince you, say so straight. This is not a whim: without an answer to that question you will drop the plan, and that has already happened.

The named minimum

Two or three tasks that survive when the cycle falls apart — work, health, life. Do only those and the pack is not failed. Two such packs in a row and we cut the load instead of pushing.

The first of each kind — by hand

The agent explains, checks and breaks. The first Dockerfile, pipeline, module, manifest and all the debugging are yours. Otherwise you end up with a working lab and zero understanding.

What is not here — and why that is a decision, not an oversight

Go and programming

You said it does not interest you. Without interest you will not do it, and a junior DevOps does not need it. Bash — only for a specific task.

Certifications are not booked

We decide on the evidence. If it turns out they really do get career changers past the HR filter — Terraform Associate after pack 07, CKA after 10. If not, we spend neither the money nor the month.

GitOps, service mesh, platform engineering

Sounds impressive and does not fit. In 11–13 hours per cycle it competes with networking and monitoring, which you do not have yet. It loses.

Nothing from scratch

Everything grows out of your lab. You already have one; building a second spends the only genuinely scarce resource.
